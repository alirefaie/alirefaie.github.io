Does Digital Literacy Prepare Nurses for Palliative Care? A 288-Respondent Study

Role: designed and executed the full statistical analysis, from raw survey export to final results.

288
nursing students surveyed
2026
fieldwork (West Bank)
SPSS
analysis environment

The problem

Palliative care depends on prepared nurses, and preparation increasingly runs through digital tools. A West Bank research study asked a direct question: is digital health literacy linked to palliative care preparedness among nursing students, and does that link hold across different student groups?

The data

A cross-sectional survey of 288 nursing students, fielded in March 2026. The raw export arrived as an online form response sheet with coded and free-text fields that needed validation before any analysis could start.

Methodology

  • Cleaned and validated the raw survey export, resolving coding inconsistencies and checking completeness case by case.
  • Tested distributions before choosing methods, then used non-parametric tests where the data did not support parametric assumptions.
  • Measured the association between digital health literacy and palliative care preparedness with correlation analysis.
  • Compared scores across demographic groups with appropriate group-comparison tests and post-hoc analysis where a global test was significant.

Results

The analysis delivered a complete correlation picture between digital health literacy and palliative care preparedness, plus demographic comparison tables showing which student groups differed meaningfully and which did not. Every result is tied to a named test, stated assumptions, and a significance level, so each number can be traced back to a justified methodological choice on clean data.

Institutional and identifying details are withheld for confidentiality. No participant-level data appears on this site.
