What Predicts Diabetes Complications? Evidence from 518 Patient Records

Role: data preparation and statistical analysis of a clinical patient survey.

518
patients in the dataset
46
clinical & demographic variables
SPSS
analysis environment

The problem

How common are the long-term complications of diabetes, and which risk factors travel with them? This project analyzed a clinical patient survey covering retinopathy, neuropathy, nephropathy, coronary heart disease, and stroke.

The data

518 patient records combining demographics, insurance coverage, medications, and laboratory results (HbA1c, lipid panel, kidney function, albumin-creatinine ratio) with clinical outcomes for five major diabetes complications. The mix of lab values, coded categories, and derived clinical flags makes this exactly the kind of dataset where careless cleaning silently changes the conclusions.

Methodology

  • Validated clinical variables against plausible ranges and resolved coding inconsistencies across demographic and insurance fields.
  • Derived analysis variables from the raw labs and exam fields, including BMI categories and blood pressure control flags.
  • Profiled the prevalence of each complication and compared risk factors between affected and unaffected patient groups.
  • Prepared clean, analysis-ready tables suitable for clinical reporting.

Results

The dataset supports a full epidemiological picture: how frequent each major complication is in this patient population, and how glycemic control, blood pressure, lipids, and kidney markers differ between patients with and without each outcome.

The dataset is fully de-identified. No patient, clinic, or physician details appear on this site.
