How Palestinian Households Spend: A National Survey Analysis

Role: full analytical pipeline on a national public survey dataset.

The problem

The Palestinian Expenditure and Consumption Survey (PECS) is the national household survey that measures how households in Palestine earn, spend, and consume. It is the kind of dataset where good analysis starts with respecting the survey design, not with running models.

The data

The 2017 round of PECS, covering 3,739 households. Working at this scale means real data quality work: inconsistent coding, derived variables that need rebuilding, and aggregates that must reconcile with published national figures.

Methodology

  • Audited the raw household file: missingness patterns, coding consistency, and out-of-range values.
  • Built household-level expenditure and consumption indicators from the raw modules.
  • Compared expenditure patterns across household groups with appropriate survey-aware summaries and group comparisons.
  • Kept the full pipeline reproducible so every reported figure can be traced back to the raw records behind it.

Results

The analysis profiles how household expenditure and consumption vary across Palestinian households, turning a complex multi-module national survey into a small set of clear, verifiable indicators.

PECS microdata is collected by the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics. This case study describes independent analysis of the dataset; no household-level records are shown on this site.
